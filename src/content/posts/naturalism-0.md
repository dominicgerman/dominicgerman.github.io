For me, I became an naturalist when I took seriously the implications of two beliefs I had long held.

1) Darwin’s theory of evolution by natural selection
2) Ghosts aren’t real

When I say naturalist, I just that there's only world, physical or natural world. I'm a materialist or physicalist or whatever you want to call me. But I wasn't always this way. 

To cut a long story short, I remember reading Sapiens during the pandemic which got me flirting with atheism. Yuval led me to Pinker who turned me onto Sean Carroll, David Deutsch, and more.

The point is that it took a while for me to realize how devastating Darwin’s discovery was to religious belief including my own. I think Sapiens really drove home the reality of the history of life on this planet. And then Dawkins, Dennett and others quickly answered all my questions. And their answers are extremely compelling.

Dennett in particular likes to point out just how shocking and revolutionary an idea Darwin's was. It was the final piece of the puzzle in explaining the universe in purely mechanical, physical terms.

The other point about ghosts not being real is more of a philosophical issue at the end of the day. It’s dualism, Occam’s razor, real patterns… all that stuff. Essentially it comes down to the fact that supernatural beings don’t exist because they don’t do anything. I am therefore in some sense, just waiting for God or ghosts or whatever to reveal themselves. But if they did, we would just include them in our description of the natural world as Sean Carroll and others have pointed out.

Deutsch has since driven my atheism into the realm of near certainty. He basically makes the point that all supernatural explanations are bad explanations by definition.

---

I think these are the only real options on offer and you only get to pick one:

1. You embrace _certain_ supernatural explanations.
2. You reject _all_ supernatural explanations.
3. You withhold judgement on the viability of any given supernatural explanation.

If you chose number one, you're a believer. If number two, you're an atheist. And if you're number three, you're probably an agnostic. I think what people generally mean when they say they're agnostic is something like "I'm skeptical of supernatural explanations but I don't want to rule them out entirely."

I can certainly understand that position. It feels less arrogant to admit that there could be things beyond our understanding. But note that in order to be consistent, the agnostic must withold judgement on _all_ supernatural explanations. The atheist rejects all supernatural explanations because they're all wrong for the same reason: namely, they're not falisfiable.

And for this reason, they can't possibly shed any light on our understanding of reality. If there's no way in principle to find out if they're wrong, then there's certainly no way one could ever find out if they're right. They are therefore bad explanations _by definition_. That doesn't mean they couldn't be true, it just means that they can never add nothing to our understanding of ourselves or the universe as a whole. But wait... if one of them is true, we wouldn't want to dismiss that theory, right? What if, say, the Judeo-Christian god really exists? Wouldn't it be a mistake to deny his existence in that case?

Well, no.

Consider the theory that we are all living in a computer simulation and that we have no way to access the "true" reality outside the simulation. This is equivalent to believing in the supernatural because in both theories, there is a fundamental limit on our knowledge of reality. If we could break through that limit, then it's not _really_ a limit. And if we really can't break through it, then that's a new law of physics. Why woudn't we categorize it as such?

> **Sidebar**: If there really is a god, how can he be sure that _he's_ not in a simulation? Think about it.

For argument's sake, let's assume Jesus was indeed physically raised from the dead. Ok, so how did that work on a physical level? The physics must be understandable _in principle_ — surely it's not a black box to God himself. Furthermore, the process couldn't have broken our known laws of physics because if they can be broken at all, then they're not really laws of physics. If you want to argue that they aren't really laws of physics, that's fine but then you might as claim 2 + 2 = 5. But in that case, it's not _really_ supernatural it's just sufficiently advanced technology.

Imagine you're at a magic show and you see the magician pull a rabbit out of his hat. If you believed that miracles are possible in theory, you're required to acknowledge the possibility that you just witnessed a miracle. If you were unfamiliar with the concept of magic shows, it would be a very natural thing to conclude. The problem with a supernatural explanation is that it's an excuse to stop thinking. It's essentially saying "Let's just give up. We probably _won't_ figure this out so it would be easier to just say that we _can't_ figure it out."
