People who deny free will want to say "you're just your genes plus your environment and that's it" and I sort of agree. But I think I would say "I, the voice inside my head, the self that I call me, I'm just my genes, my environment, my thoughts, my culture, my thoughts about my culture, my thoughts about my thoughts about my culture..." Yeah I'm *just* all those things. 

Could we say I'm actually all the things in the universe influencing each other and creating the micro-state that I'm a part of in this moment? I'm just the effect of every cause up until now, nothing more? I'm just an NPC in the video game of the universe?

Right, well, everything is *just that*. So what? My dog is as much that as I am. But we're different he and I. For one, he isn't his thoughts. He doesn't have thoughts because he doesn't have language. He isn't his culture because he doesn't have that either. He can't ask questions, he can't even prevent himself from eating food off the table. I can prevent him from doing that by training him. But he can't train himself. Yet I can train myself.

I can train myself to want certain things and not other things. I can train myself to brush my teeth every night and floss. I'm not saying it's easy but I can do it. My dog can't do that.

So whatever it is that we have that animals don't, that's free will. If you want to call that something else, fine. I don't care what we call it but we should admit that it's real and that it enables us to hold people accountable for their actions.

Free will is only a hot button issue with people who like to deny free will. And they are almost always obsessed with libertarian free will. They only want to talk about that because they think that's the *real* free will. That's what *most people* think free will is, they say, and compatabilists are just changing the subject or being dishonest. 

Well, maybe most people do have a vaguely libertarian version of free will in their head. But so what? The important thing is that we all recognize that humans can be held responsible for their actions. And that the only time they shouldn't be held responsible is when we can point to a real anomaly in someone's physiology or psychology. 

You're not a disembodied locus of control that can magically affect your body's behavior independent of the laws of physics. You're just a bunch of atoms that can non-magically affect themselves in ways no other species can. You're special compared to other animals so we hold you to a higher standard.

If the person believes in free will and they're *not* an atheist, well then they've got much bigger issues to sort out before we can have a discussion about free will. And therefore there's no reason to engage this kind of person on the subject. There's no reason to use the denial of "free will" as this big atheist talking point. All this shit about determinism and we're all just puppets and on and on... that's just nonsense. It's one of Sam Harris' biggest blind spots in my opinion. Alex O'Connor as well although I recently heard him say that he's not a materialist (physicalist) so now it makes more sense. I must say I lost a little bit of respect for him after hearing him say that. Maybe the people who think he's only a few arguments away from theism have a point.

Anyway, there is no God, there is no libertarian free will, just the laws of physics. You are a member of a species of primates with overdeveloped brains that rule their home planet with no apparent competition for light years in all directions. No other type of thing in the universe has as much control over itself as you do. And that's pretty fucking cool.
